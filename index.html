B7:Soil Moisture–ET Balance

A stabilising feedback. Soil moisture enables evapotranspiration, but ET depletes the soil moisture store. As moisture decreases, ET is constrained — preventing runaway evaporation and setting the natural limit to transpiration-based cooling. This is the mechanism behind the Blue Water Trade-off (Mankin et al., 2018; Makarieva, 2026): in water-limited catchments, increased vegetation can increase ET demand faster than infiltration gains supply it, reducing streamflow.

Tragedy of the Commons (Atmosphere)

The atmosphere's heat-regulating capacity — cloud albedo (n11), regional moisture convergence (n12), and freedom from hazes (n16) — is a shared commons. Each individual land degradation decision (n5) adds marginally to aerosol loading and haze (R6 loop), warming the atmosphere for everyone. No single actor bears the full cost. Conversely, large-scale ecosystem restoration generates cloud albedo and biotic pump benefits that accrue globally — a positive-sum commons restoration problem requiring coordinated action at bioregional and continental scales.

Water that does not infiltrate and becomes surface runoff or streamflow — the 'blue water' fraction. In water-limited catchments, increased vegetation siphons water from streamflow to ET (Blue Water Trade-off, Mankin et al. 2018; Makarieva 2026 — China re-greening data).
Healthy vegetation creates low-pressure zones through transpiration and biological activity — the Biotic Pump effect (Makarieva, 2026). These draw in moist oceanic air, increasing regional moisture convergence. The Amazon drives continental moisture deep inland via this mechanism.
R1:Soil Carbon Sponge Regeneration

The foundational virtuous cycle. More soil organic matter creates better pore structure and water retention, which supports more plant growth and root biomass, which feeds more carbon back into the soil. Once activated by regenerative practices, this loop is self-accelerating. At 10 tC/ha/yr sequestration across 2 billion hectares, this loop could reverse global atmospheric CO₂ accumulation (Jehne, 2019).
